Listed but Not Recognised: Diacritics and Label Provenance in African Language Identification In Preparation

Victor Miene

Target: ACL 2027

African-language datasets are built by trusting a language identifier's labels, but a listed label does not mean the language is recognised. Testing 9 identifiers on natural text in 20 African languages, I found that a label can hold another language, and a correctly labelled class can fail when its training text is marked differently from how people write. Where a language is not recognised, tone marks lead identifiers to label its text as Yoruba or another better-recognised language that uses the same marks, while placebo marks do not.
